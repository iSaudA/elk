# AYN AL-SIJILL promotional film research

Research date: 25 September 2026
Target: a 30-second prospective-client film for Azure-hosted operational observability

## Executive conclusion

The current film is competent motion design, but it reads as an animated sales deck. The high-end references below behave more like advertising: they earn attention with one surprising idea, make the product tangible through two or three precise proofs, and close before the idea loses force.

The remake should be built around one visual and narrative concept:

> **The KPI stayed green. The customer journey broke.**

A successful payment becomes a luminous order token moving through a dark, cinematic system. It passes the green KPI, then disappears into an invisible gap before the order is created. AYN AL-SIJILL exposes that gap, connects the evidence, and identifies the cause. Azure is the trusted environment, not a checklist scene.

The recommended formula is **one promise, three proofs, one decisive close**:

1. Promise: see the failure hidden behind a healthy KPI.
2. Proof 1: detect the missing order event.
3. Proof 2: correlate payment, order, and database evidence through one trace.
4. Proof 3: deliver the incident context needed to act.
5. Close: see the Ghost Order live in Azure.

## Reference set

The list prioritizes official company channels and first-party case studies written by the studios that made the work. Exact runtimes are included only when the first-party source exposes them.

### 1. Artlist, Official Big Game Commercial 2026, 30 seconds

Sources: [official film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yqxaKfMss4Q), [Artlist making-of](https://artlist.io/blog/super-bowl-ads-2026-artlist/)

The film opens inside the visual language of current Big Game commercials, then reveals that the polished spot was made with Artlist AI in five days. The film itself is the product proof. Rapid live action, AI, and animation shots are held together by sound and by the concept, not by a common dashboard layout. Explanatory copy is minimal, followed by a hard proposition turn and clean brand close.

**Lesson:** make the film demonstrate the claim. Our film should not merely say that AYN AL-SIJILL correlates operational evidence. The transaction shown in the opening should become the exact trace and incident shown later.

**Lesson:** every transition should express the system's behavior. For AYN AL-SIJILL, a trace line should not be decoration. It should be the device that connects scenes, reveals evidence, and resolves the story.

## What makes the strongest films feel expensive

### Concept and narrative arc

The best films can be summarized as one sentence with a visual verb: silos collaborate, creatures evolve, chaos gathers, a sketchbook comes alive, or reality amplifies. They do not begin with the product architecture. A familiar problem or surprising world creates tension, the product changes that state, and the final brand frame names the transformation.

For a 30-second software film, the reliable structure is:

- 0 to 3 seconds: visual contradiction or consequence
- 3 to 9 seconds: deepen the buyer pain
- 9 to 21 seconds: two or three product proofs
- 21 to 26 seconds: trusted environment or operational outcome
- 26 to 30 seconds: brand, proposition, and one CTA

### Opening hook

High-end work begins with movement, tension, comedy, or sensory surprise. It rarely starts with a logo, category label, or abstract corporate sentence. The opening needs to be understandable with the sound off and intriguing before the viewer knows the product name.

### Typography and copy density

Typography is kinetic and semantic. It bends, splits, gathers, or becomes an interface element. Copy arrives in short, high-contrast phrases. Paragraphs, persistent labels, numbered stages, and several equal-weight captions reduce the sense of confidence.

A 30-second film should usually stay near 25 to 40 on-screen words, excluding authentic UI labels. The audience should never have to decide whether to read a heading, paragraph, rail label, chip, and dashboard at the same time.

### Product UI treatment

Premium films use UI selectively. They crop into one action, preserve real proportions and states, and move the camera only to direct attention. Full dashboards appear only when the dashboard itself is the payoff. UI is presented after the audience understands why it matters.

### 2D, 3D, and cinematic language

The references mix techniques instead of treating 2D and 3D as separate scenes. Tactile materials, depth of field, controlled reflections, macro scale, realistic shadows, and motivated camera movement give invisible software behavior physical consequence. 3D is most effective when it embodies a specific idea, not when it is added as generic technological spectacle.

### Edit rhythm and transitions

Strong transitions carry meaning forward. A circle becomes the next search, a workspace crushes the silos, or one object multiplies and recombines. The rhythm alternates anticipation and release. Uniform four- or five-second scenes with the same reveal animation feel like slides.

### Sound design and music

The premium references consistently credit original music, sound design, or a dedicated audio studio. Sound explains scale and causality: a payment confirmation can be crisp and reassuring, the missing order can remove the low end, correlation can add synchronized clicks, and root cause can land with one controlled impact. A music bed alone cannot provide this narrative precision.

### Branding and CTA

Branding comes from the visual system throughout the film, not a logo repeated in a header. The close is given three to five seconds of calm after the fastest section. It uses one proposition and one action.

## Diagnosis of the current AYN AL-SIJILL film

The current source is a 900-frame Remotion composition with six discrete sequences: intro, risk, detect, correlate, Azure, and final. Each sequence fades in and out inside the same framed HUD treatment. It uses persistent top and bottom rails, a background grid, an animated scan line, stage labels, headings, explanatory paragraphs, chips, and dashboard cards. The principal copy includes “Every KPI is a promise,” “The risk is what your dashboard misses,” “See the full journey,” “One trace. One cause,” “Built on Azure. Protected by design,” and “Protect the KPIs your clients count on.” See [`video/src/ayn-promo.tsx`](../video/src/ayn-promo.tsx) and [`video/README.md`](../video/README.md).

What works:

- The Ghost Order is a real and relevant scenario.
- The film has a logical problem-to-detection-to-correlation flow.
- The palette is controlled and the hierarchy is readable.
- It identifies concrete evidence: payment success, missing order, trace ID, database timeout, and an incident notification.

What is missing:

1. **No arresting first image.** “Every KPI is a promise” is polished corporate copy, but it is an assertion rather than an event. Nothing impossible, emotional, funny, or tactile happens in the first three seconds.
2. **No ownable visual metaphor.** Grids, glowing dots, pipeline nodes, and dark panels belong to the general category of observability software. They do not belong uniquely to AYN AL-SIJILL.
3. **The film behaves like six slides.** Every scene occupies its own box and fades away. Information advances, but visual energy does not accumulate from shot to shot.
4. **Too many simultaneous reading targets.** Rails, stage numbers, section labels, headlines, paragraphs, chips, timestamps, and cards compete. The viewer reads the film instead of feeling it.
5. **UI without enough authenticity.** The designed cards are clean but generic. They do not yet provide the trust of tightly cropped real Kibana, Telegram, Azure, or Power BI surfaces.
6. **Flat depth and camera language.** Most movement is opacity, translation, scale, and a travelling line inside a fixed 1920 by 1080 canvas. There is little macro scale, parallax, material change, lens behavior, or spatial reveal.
7. **Sound is a bed, not a narrative system.** The composition mixes one instrumental track. There are no synchronized transaction, absence, correlation, alert, or resolution cues.
8. **Azure is presented as a list.** Five nodes and security chips explain an architecture but interrupt the customer story. The buyer benefit is trust and deployability. The full stack belongs in a technical follow-up, not the emotional peak of a 30-second ad.
9. **The close overclaims the present implementation.** The repository describes a course-demo commerce simulator with synthetic events and no real payments or customers. It detects and alerts; it does not automatically prevent or remediate the Ghost Order. “Protect your clients” should therefore be positioned as the prospective value of production integration, not an already validated outcome.

In short, the missing ingredient is not more animation. It is **creative compression**: one memorable idea, one continuous visual transformation, less copy, genuine product proof, and authored sound.

## Recommended remake: “THE KPI STAYED GREEN”

### Single-minded proposition

**See the failure behind the KPI.**

The film sells operational truth, not a logging stack. AYN AL-SIJILL connects business events across a client journey so teams can detect hidden failures, trace their cause, and act with context.

### Hero metaphor

A single luminous order token moves through a sculptural Azure-hosted system. `PAYMENT APPROVED` turns the token green and pushes the KPI upward. The token then reaches a near-invisible gap where `ORDER CREATED` should be and falls out of the journey. The KPI remains green. The trace, represented by the AYN line or lens, dives after the missing token, gathers scattered event fragments, and pulls them into one cause.

This gives the film:

- one hero object: the order token
- one contradiction: green KPI, broken journey
- one transition device: the continuous trace line
- one material system: smoked glass, cobalt light, mint success, coral failure
- one camera journey: macro follow, fall, investigation, resolve

### Copy discipline

Recommended campaign copy:

- `PAID.`
- `But no order.`
- `The KPI stayed green.`
- `AYN AL-SIJILL sees the gap.`
- `Detect. Correlate. Explain.`
- `Context to act.`
- `Deployed on Azure.`
- `See what the KPI misses.`
- `Book a live Ghost Order walkthrough.`

This is 29 words before the CTA and product UI labels. Avoid a voiceover unless a strong bilingual Arabic and English performance is available. The concept is designed to work silently with captions and become more cinematic with sound.

### Visual direction

- **Mood:** precise, architectural, calm under pressure. Avoid the common neon cyber-control-room look.
- **Materials:** smoked glass, dark anodized metal, subtle volumetric haze, controlled reflections, and physically believable light.
- **Color:** retain cobalt as the AYN signature, mint only for confirmed success, coral only for missing or failed evidence. Do not wash whole scenes in accent color.
- **Typography:** one distinctive display sans plus a technical mono used sparingly for real identifiers. Remove persistent rails and stage numbers.
- **Camera:** one continuous forward journey with two deliberate changes of scale, transaction macro to system wide, then evidence macro. Use cuts only on causally linked sync points.
- **UI:** capture real, sanitized product states at final delivery size. Crop to the four facts that matter. Never show an entire dashboard merely to prove that a dashboard exists.
- **Branding:** introduce AYN only when the trace activates. Let the lens or eye geometry become the correlation device so the mark has narrative meaning.

### Audio direction

Commission or build a 30-second score to picture. Start with a restrained, premium pulse at approximately 105 to 115 BPM, create a vacuum at the missing order, then rebuild the rhythm from event clicks and trace locks. The alert should feel authoritative, not alarming. The final two seconds need a short sonic signature derived from the confirmation tone heard at the start.

The mix should include at least these authored events:

1. payment confirmation
2. missing-event vacuum
3. falling token impact
4. trace activation
5. three evidence locks
6. incident arrival
7. Azure trust lift
8. brand resolve

### Claims and credibility guardrail

The current repository is a validated Azure demo, not a production deployment against a client's commerce platform. The film may truthfully show:

- synthetic checkout scenarios running on Azure Functions
- authenticated event ingestion over HTTPS
- correlated order, trace, and transaction identifiers
- operational evidence in Elasticsearch/Kibana
- a reporting copy in Azure SQL for Power BI
- Telegram incident alerts with cause, impact, and trace context
- Azure-hosted infrastructure with Key Vault-backed alert credentials and restricted access controls

Until a real client integration and operational outcome are validated, avoid:

- “prevents lost orders”
- “protects every client”
- “guarantees KPI accuracy”
- “production-grade”
- an implied automated remediation

Prefer:

- “See the failure behind the KPI.”
- “Connect operational evidence across the client journey.”
- “Detect hidden failures and trace the cause.”
- “Designed for deployment in your Azure environment,” if that deployment model is part of the offer.
- “Book a live Ghost Order walkthrough.”

## Production recommendation

Do not begin the remake by coding six new Remotion scenes. Begin with three artifacts:

1. **A black-and-white animatic** with the full 30-second timing and final copy.
2. **Three styleframes** at 0:04, 0:16, and 0:28 that prove the metaphor, UI integration, and close.
3. **A scored audio sketch** with all eight narrative sound events before final animation.

Only proceed to full production when the animatic works with no color and the story is clear with the sound muted. Produce the hero token and continuous trace as reusable systems, then composite sanitized real UI as evidence. The final output should include 16:9, 1:1, and 9:16 versions planned from the same safe composition, plus 6-second and 15-second cutdowns.

## Acceptance bar for the new film

The film is ready only if:

- a new viewer can state the problem and value in one sentence after one viewing
- the first meaningful visual event occurs in the first second
- no frame asks the viewer to read a paragraph
- every technical label on screen proves a claim
- the order token and trace create a continuous causal story
- the film remains understandable without sound
- the sound mix still tells tension, investigation, and resolution without picture
- the last brand and CTA frame holds for at least three seconds
- all product claims match the demonstrated repository scope
